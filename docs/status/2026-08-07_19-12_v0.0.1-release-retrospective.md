## g) Questions I Cannot Answer Myself

### 1. Is `wrapcheck` in response.go accepted by design or a real issue?

The `Response` methods return `stream.Send()` errors directly without wrapping. AGENTS.md documents that `erraudit`'s `generic_return` warnings are "accepted by design" because this is a library. But it says nothing about `wrapcheck`. Are the 11 `wrapcheck` warnings on `response.go:37/48/61/72/77/83/88/103` intentional (same pattern as the accepted `generic_return`) or should `stream.Send` errors be wrapped with `errorfamily`?

> **Resolution:** Accepted by design. v0.0.2 added `stream.Send` to wrapcheck ignore-sigs in `.golangci.yml`.

### 2. Should the `go 1.26.5` in go.mod be `go 1.26` instead?

The patch version (`.5`) in go.mod means consumers need at least Go 1.26.5. golangci-lint can't handle this. Is there a specific reason for `.5` (a language feature, a stdlib fix) or is it just the installed toolchain version that leaked into go.mod? Lowering to `go 1.26` would improve compatibility and unblock golangci-lint.

> **Resolution:** Lowered to `go 1.26` in v0.0.2.

### 3. Should I re-tag v0.0.1 or release v0.0.2?

The v0.0.1 tag points at a commit with a failing CI. Options:

- **Patch in place**: fix CI, force-move the `v0.0.1` tag (destructive, but no one has consumed it yet)
- **Release v0.0.2**: leave v0.0.1 broken, cut a clean v0.0.2 immediately
- **Leave it**: fix CI going forward, accept v0.0.1 had a broken lint job

This depends on whether you care about v0.0.1 being a "clean" tag for SemVer history.

> **Resolution:** Released v0.0.2 with CI green. v0.0.1 left as historical artifact.
